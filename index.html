Open Loot.

01

The Problem

Open Loot didn't exist. Big Time, the PC RPG it was meant to host, was just starting development. The brief was to design a Web3 NFT marketplace from scratch, with no internal patterns or precedent, conceived from day one as a multi-game platform rather than a single-title catalog.

02

Constraints

A small team (one designer), a tight and shifting timeline (6 months stretching to a year), no design system or formal user testing in the early stages. Research leaned on OpenSea, Magic Eden, and NBA Top Shot to understand catalog, checkout, and wallet-connection patterns in high-volume NFT products.

02

03

The Decision

With no research data of our own yet, the criterion was usability in service of a business goal: checkout in 1-2 clicks from any product screen, desktop-first.

That rule, not a design system (which came later), is what drove every layout and flow decision.

The checkout target that drove every layout decision.

03

04

Process

Each feature had it's own sole project, simple UX to review options and consider resources to estimate. Once move into mockups, internal testing with QA in staging, and Customer Service as a live research channel. Sprint cadence in Jira, weekly per-feature syncs with a lean team: PM, engineering, QA, and me.

Design System

End-to-end design of every feature, resource scoping alongside PM and engineering, and identifying a bottleneck that led to designing a self-service portal for partners, so each studio could mint assets and manage its own content without straining engineering.

Simply Vision.

into a picture of how you actually live.

How it works

Track a little,
learn a lot.

Every day you log quick check-ins across five parts of life — Sleep, Nutrition, Work, Social and General Mood. Each takes seconds: how much, and how it felt.

The app then reflects it straight back. A Track screen captures the moment; an Analyze screen turns it into a clear, colour-coded result — trends, streaks, personal records and a running happiness index.

So the patterns behind how you feel stop being vague and become something you can actually see, compare and act on.

Track (left) feeds Analyze (right) — Nutrition & Sleep
The system

A colour for
every feeling.

The whole product speaks one colour language. Each of the six life categories owns a hue; every hue runs down a value scale — from “Great” to “Terrible” — so a screen’s mood is legible before a single word is read, and it stays consistent across tracking, results and charts.

Every screen,

A dense product needed a clear map. The flow charts each mode and stage — track, analyze, friends, records — and studies how quickly a new user reaches the app’s value, with onboarding tuned so the first real insight never feels far away.

The project

Insurance,
without the dread.

Banco Patagonia and SURA wanted a portal where anyone could quote and buy insurance — home, life, pet, personal accident and more — 100% online.

Insurance is famously tedious: dense policies, endless forms, jargon people never stop to read. The brief was to make it feel light.

So we built a guided experience that leads people step by step, keeps the focus on benefits instead of paperwork, and works as comfortably on a phone as on a desktop.

Foundations

A friendlier
face for finance.

The bank’s teal-to-blue identity anchors trust; a bright lime accent keeps it approachable. On top, a set of simple, rounded product icons makes each type of cover instantly legible — less structured and formal, closer to everyday people.

Sitemap & user flow

One clear
path to covered.

The whole journey was mapped as a single spine — from picking a product through to a contracted policy. The branches (home, common, employees) fold back into one shared checkout: select, address, summary, payment, done.

The flow

Five steps,
no surprises.

A persistent stepper — construction type, plans, address, summary, payment — tells people exactly where they are and what’s left. Plans sit side by side with only what matters: coverage and price, nothing to decode.

The payoff

Covered,
in a few taps.

The journey ends where insurance usually begins its paperwork: a plain, reassuring confirmation — you’re insured, here’s your request number — instead of a maze of PDFs.

Modeling + Render

Introduction

This project explores the integration of a single-family home within the rugged landscape of Patagonia. The primary challenge was to translate the warmth of noble materials—such as wood and stone—into a contemporary architectural language that respects the scale of the surroundings.

We focused on a simple yet efficient volumetric design, prioritizing natural light intake and panoramic views of the mountain range. This presentation details the technical journey from initial drafting to final construction.

_ PROJECTHouse in the mountains
_ LOCATIONChubut, Patagonia, Argentina
_ REQUESTSketchup + V-Ray/Enscape + Photoshop
_ YEAR2025
01
Project

Development of comprehensive technical documentation, including architectural floor plans and longitudinal sections to define internal spatiality. The design utilizes a modular grid system to optimize construction resources in remote geographical locations.

02
Modeling

The 3D modeling phase was executed with technical precision, transforming architectural schemes into a detailed digital structure. Special emphasis was placed on the structural accuracy of the timber joints and the site’s topography to ensure a realistic environment.

Through wireframes and volumetric models, we validated proportions and solar incidence on the facades, allowing for dynamic adjustments before moving into the texturing and post-processing stages.

03
Renders

Photorealistic visualization designed to capture the atmosphere of the retreat under various lighting conditions. Global illumination was carefully tuned to highlight natural textures and the contrast between interior warmth and the exterior climate.

04
Results

The transition from the digital model to the physical build demonstrates the viability of the proposed design. On-site monitoring allowed us to compare the 3D model’s precision with traditional construction techniques applied in the region.
